AURORA LABS INFORMATION SECURITY POLICY

Effective date: 1 January 2026
Owner: Security Engineering
Approved by: the security committee

This policy sets the minimum security requirements for every person who uses Aurora
Labs systems or handles Aurora Labs data. It applies to employees, contractors and
interns. Local addenda may add requirements but may not remove any.


1. PURPOSE AND SCOPE

Aurora Labs builds software that processes customer data on behalf of logistics
companies. A security failure at Aurora Labs can expose that customer data, so the
rules in this policy exist to reduce the chance of unauthorised access and to limit
the damage when something does go wrong.

The policy covers every company owned device, every cloud service the company
operates, and every remote connection to the company network. It applies during
working hours and outside them, because an attacker does not keep office hours.


2. PASSWORD RULES

2.1 Minimum standards

Every password used for an Aurora Labs account must be at least fourteen characters
long. Passphrases of three or more unrelated words are encouraged. Passwords must
not contain the employee's name, employee number or the word aurora.

2.2 Uniqueness

A password used for an Aurora Labs account must not be reused on any other service.
The company provides a password manager to every employee at no cost. All work
credentials must be stored in it.

2.3 Rotation

Passwords are rotated every twelve months. A password must be changed immediately if
there is any suspicion that it has been disclosed, if the employee sees a login from
an unfamiliar location, or if the security team requests a change. Forced rotation
notifications arrive from the identity provider and never from email.


3. MULTI FACTOR AUTHENTICATION

3.1 Where it is required

Multi factor authentication is mandatory for email, the code repository, the cloud
console, the expense system and every internal application reachable through the
intranet. There are no exceptions to this list.

3.2 Approved factors

Hardware security keys registered through the identity provider are the preferred
factor and are issued to every employee. Time based one time password applications
are acceptable for services that do not support hardware keys.

3.3 Disallowed factors

Short message service codes are not an approved factor for any Aurora Labs service,
because they can be intercepted through SIM swapping. Push approval prompts are
permitted only for the virtual private network client, and only as a second factor
after the primary factor has already succeeded.


4. DEVICE ENROLMENT

4.1 Company laptops

Every company laptop must be enrolled in mobile device management within five
business days of the employee receiving the device. Enrolment is completed by the
employee through the company portal and installs full disk encryption, the endpoint
agent and the automatic screen lock policy.

4.2 Personal devices

Any personal phone or tablet used to read company email or to access internal
applications must be enrolled in mobile device management within ten business days of
first use. Enrolment of a personal device installs only the mail profile and the
remote wipe certificate for company data. It does not install the endpoint agent.

4.3 Lost devices

A lost or stolen company device must be reported to the security team within one hour
of discovery. The security team revokes sessions and, for company laptops, issues a
remote wipe command. Reports go to the security team through the incident form
described in section 6, not by email.


5. DATA CLASSIFICATION

All Aurora Labs data must be classified into one of four levels before it is stored
in a new system.

5.1 Public

Public data is intended for anyone. Marketing pages, published pricing and the
public status page are public. No special handling is required.

5.2 Internal

Internal data is not secret but should not leave the company. Org charts, meeting
notes and internal announcements are internal. Internal data may be shared with any
employee and with contractors under a signed agreement.

5.3 Confidential

Confidential data causes harm to a customer or to Aurora Labs if it leaks. Customer
contracts, production data exports and financial forecasts are confidential.
Confidential data must be stored only in systems approved by the security team, must
be encrypted in transit, and must never be pasted into a public chat channel or a
personal note taking application.

5.4 Restricted

Restricted data is the most sensitive level and includes authentication secrets,
private keys and any dataset containing personal data of identifiable individuals.
Restricted data must be stored only in the secrets manager or the designated data
store. Access is granted per named individual, reviewed every ninety days, and
logged. Restricted data must never be copied to a laptop, a removable drive or a
consumer cloud storage service.


6. INCIDENT REPORTING

6.1 What counts as an incident

An incident is any suspected or confirmed unauthorised access, loss, disclosure or
destruction of company data, any successful phishing attempt against an employee, and
any malware infection on a company device. If an employee is unsure whether an event
counts, it counts and should be reported.

6.2 Reporting deadline

A suspected incident must be reported within one hour of discovery. Reports are made
through the incident form in the security portal. The form reaches the on call
security engineer immediately, at any hour.

6.3 What to include

The report must state what was observed, when it was observed, which systems or data
may be affected, and any action already taken. An employee who has accidentally
clicked a link must say so plainly. Delay caused by hesitation increases the damage;
the company does not discipline employees for reporting promptly and honestly.

6.4 Triage and follow up

The on call engineer triages the report within four hours and assigns a severity.
Severity one incidents trigger a written post mortem within five business days of
closure. Severity two incidents trigger a written post mortem within ten business
days. Lower severities are reviewed in the monthly security meeting.


7. REMOTE ACCESS

7.1 Virtual private network

Every connection to an internal Aurora Labs system must go through the company
virtual private network. Direct exposure of an internal service to the public
internet is not permitted, and no internal hostname resolves from outside the VPN.

7.2 Client configuration

Split tunnelling is disabled on the company VPN client. When the client is connected,
all traffic routes through the tunnel. Employees must keep the client up to date and
must install a client update within seven days of a release.

7.3 Public networks

On public wireless networks, employees must connect through the VPN before opening
any internal application. Employees should prefer mobile hotspots over open wireless
networks when travelling.


8. CLOUD AND SAAS USAGE

8.1 Approved services

Aurora Labs systems must be hosted only in the cloud accounts registered with the
security team. Creating a new cloud account, signing up for a new software as a
service product with company data, or moving data between regions requires prior
approval from the security team. Unapproved shadow services discovered during audit
are decommissioned.

8.2 Credential handling

Cloud console access is granted through single sign on and is never shared. Long
lived access keys are prohibited where a short lived token can be used, and any
access key that must exist must be stored in the secrets manager and rotated every
ninety days. Service accounts are reviewed quarterly and any account unused for one
hundred and eighty days is disabled.

8.3 Customer data exports

A production data export containing customer records may be taken only through the
approved export pipeline, which writes to the restricted data store and records the
purpose. A copy to a laptop, a spreadsheet on a personal device or a consumer file
sharing service is a policy violation and is treated as an incident under section 6.


9. PHISHING AND SOCIAL ENGINEERING

9.1 Recognising attempts

Employees will occasionally receive messages that imitate Aurora Labs or a vendor and
seek credentials, payment or a file transfer. Common signs are an unusual sender
address, a request to bypass a normal process, urgency or threat language, and a
link whose domain does not match the vendor's real domain.

9.2 Verification of payment changes

Any message requesting a change to bank details or a payment to a new account must be
verified by a second channel, typically a phone call to a known number, before any
action is taken. This applies to messages that appear to come from an executive.

9.3 Reporting

Suspected phishing messages must be reported within one hour using the report phishing
button in the mail client. The message must not be forwarded manually to colleagues,
because forwarding spreads the attempt. The security team quarantines similar
messages across all mailboxes after a single report.


10. TRAINING

10.1 Onboarding

Every new employee completes the security onboarding module within their first five
business days. The module covers this policy, the phishing reporting process and the
data classification levels.

10.2 Ongoing training

All employees complete an annual security refresher. Employees who handle restricted
data complete an additional module every six months. Completion is tracked by the
security team and non completion is escalated to the employee's manager after the
deadline.


11. PHYSICAL SECURITY

Laptops must not be left unattended in a public place. When working from a public
location, the screen should be positioned away from passers by and the laptop must
never be checked into hold luggage in an unlocked container. Company documents
printed at home must be shredded or destroyed when no longer needed. Access badges
for office buildings must be reported lost within one hour, on the same timeline as
a lost device under section 4.3.


12. POLICY EXCEPTIONS

An exception to any rule in this policy requires a written request to the security
committee. The committee reviews exceptions monthly. An approved exception states a
business reason, an expiry date and a compensating control, and no exception is
valid for longer than twelve months without a fresh review.


13. REVIEW

This policy is reviewed every twelve months by the security committee, or sooner if
a material change occurs in the company's systems or in applicable law.
